We don't sell gloves yet, on purpose. We won't put our name on a pair until they're as good as the gloves serious keepers already wear. That also means we've got no reason to push you towards any brand. Here's how to choose.

## 1. Surface first

- **Natural grass (dry or wet):** any latex works. Softer latex grips best.
- **Artificial grass (3G/4G):** rubber crumb wears latex fast. Use tougher training latex, or accept that soft match gloves won't last long.
- **Indoor/hard courts:** abrasion is the problem. Use tougher latex, and be wary of anything with a very soft palm.

If you play on more than one surface, the best fix isn't a magic glove. It's **two pairs**.

## 2. Cut: how the glove fits your hand

| Cut | Feels like | Good for | Trade-off |
|-----|-----------|----------|-----------|
| **Negative** | Tight, seams on the inside | Feel and control. Popular with keepers who like a close fit. | Less latex contact area. Can feel tight at first. |
| **Roll finger** | Latex wraps around the fingers | Maximum latex contact when catching | Slightly less snug feel |
| **Flat palm** | Traditional, a little roomier | Comfort, wider hands | Less close control |
| **Hybrid** | Mixes of the above | Getting the best of two cuts | Depends heavily on the brand |

There's no "best" cut. Try what feels natural when your hand is in a catching shape.

## 3. Latex: soft grips, hard lasts

- **Soft/"contact" latex:** maximum grip, wears fastest. Best for matches on grass.
- **Harder/training latex:** less grip, lasts much longer. Best for training and turf.
- **Thickness:** thicker latex cushions more and usually lasts longer, but reduces feel.

## 4. Fingersaves (spines)

Stiffeners that stop fingers bending backwards. They're useful if you've had finger injuries or are recovering from one. Many keepers find they reduce feel and flexibility, so don't buy them "just in case" unless that trade-off suits you.

## 5. Wrist

- **Strap (hook-and-loop):** adjustable, secure, the most common.
- **Bandage/wrap style:** very secure wrist support.
- **Strapless/sock-style:** light, and easy on and off. Less adjustable.

## 6. Size — measure, don't guess

Measure around your hand below the knuckles (thumb out). Your glove size is roughly that measurement in inches. Brands vary by up to half a size, so check the brand chart too. [Use our size calculator →](/pages/size-guide)

## What's worth paying for (and what isn't)

- **Worth it:** good latex on your match gloves, a proper fit, and a second pair for training.
- **Often not worth it:** top-of-range gloves for training on turf, fingersaves you don't need, or buying a size up "to grow into" (a loose glove costs you catches).
