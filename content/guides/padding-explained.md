Padding does one job: it spreads the impact when you land. More padding isn't automatically better, because it adds bulk and heat. The right answer depends on where you play and how you fall.

## The zones

| Zone | Protects | You want it if… |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| **Hips** | The side of the pelvis and hip bone, which you land on after a sideways dive | Almost everyone who dives. This is the one to start with. |
| **Tailbone** | The base of the spine | You play on hard, frozen or artificial pitches, or fall backwards a lot |
| **Thighs** | The outer thigh | You play on turf or hard ground, where the side of the leg takes friction and impact |
| **Knees** | Kneecaps and knee sides | You play on turf or indoors. Pants only. |
| **Elbows** | Elbow point and forearm | You make a lot of low saves on turf or hard ground. Long-sleeve jerseys only. |

## Shorts vs pants

- **Shorts:** lighter and cooler. Best on grass in normal weather.
- **Pants:** cover knees and the full leg. Best for turf, indoor floors and cold weather.
- **Base layer vs outer layer:** padded undershorts go under normal kit shorts. Padded outer shorts are the kit.

## Fit matters more than foam

A pad that isn't on your hip when you land does nothing. Padded shorts and pants should fit **snug**. If you're between sizes and slim, go down. [Size guide →](/pages/size-guide#apparel)

## Trade-offs, honestly

- More zones means more heat. In summer on grass, hips alone is usually enough.
- Thicker foam cushions more but feels bulkier when you dive.
- Knee pads on grass are usually overkill. On turf, they save your skin.
